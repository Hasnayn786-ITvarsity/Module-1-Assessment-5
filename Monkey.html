MONKEY

Monkeys are clever, playful primates that live all over the world in many different habitats. There are over 260 different species of monkeys, divided into Old World monkeys from Africa and Asia and New World monkeys from Central and South America. They range from tiny marmosets that fit in your hand to large baboons. Most monkeys live in trees and are very agile, with long tails for balance and hands that can grip and manipulate objects. A typical monkey is highly social and lives in troops where they constantly groom, play and look out for each other.

Monkeys are known for their intelligence and curiosity. They can use tools, solve puzzles, and learn quickly by watching others. Many species have cheek pouches to store food like a shopping bag, so they can collect food and eat it later safely. Their expressive faces and loud calls are how they communicate - from screeches and howls to chattering. Some New World monkeys even have a prehensile tail that works like a fifth hand to grab branches and hold them while they eat.

They are omnivores, eating fruits, leaves, insects, nuts and sometimes small animals. They live in large troops of 20 to 100 individuals with a strict social hierarchy. They spend their days foraging, playing and resting in the trees, and are most active in the early morning and late afternoon.
